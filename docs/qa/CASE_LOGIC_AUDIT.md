## Clockwork Garden

**Intended solution:** Emil Hart used the service hatch during the timed blackout and took the Atlas watch; the dealer’s letter establishes the private-sale motive.

**Reasoning chain:** The sealed archive key rules out ordinary cabinet access. The service hatch connects the archive to the potting room, and clay/brass traces place recent activity on that route. The fuse timer establishes a planned 82-second blackout; potting soil on its casing is consistent with the potting-room route. Emil corrected the old floor plan the day before the theft, and the dealer’s letter is signed E.H. while naming an overdue contract payment. Emil’s profile now states that he had a delayed contract payment, linking that motive clue to him. Together these clues support a preplanned bypass and a private buyer.

| Suspect | Opportunity | Motive | Alibi / contradiction | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Evelyn Shaw | Conservator with archive knowledge | Says the watch belongs in a public museum | Humidity log entry at 9:15 is before the 9:17 blackout; she reports the loss early | Possible access, but no private-sale link; her account is not a complete time alibi |
| Theo Marr | Guard knows alarms; east-gate sensor records 9:16 and 9:19 | Rent notice | Interior service corridors lack camera coverage | Financial pressure exists, but no clue ties him to the map-room letter or floor-plan correction |
| Emil Hart | Corrected the floor plan; hatch supplies an unkeyed route | E.H. letter and delayed contract payment | Map-room account has no time-stamped log | Strongest combined method and motive evidence; intended culprit |
| Dr. Ada Solis | Horologist who once offered to buy the watch | Private collection | Reception host recalls her in the glass hall during the outage | Motive is plausible, but the host’s account and declined offer weigh against her; no route clue links her to the hatch |

**Alternatives and assumptions:** Evelyn’s 9:15 entry is not a continuous alibi, and the gate sensor does not by itself prove Theo could not leave the gate. These are weaker alternatives rather than fully measured alibis. The unique intended answer is supported by the combined E.H. letter, delayed payment, and Emil’s floor-plan correction. The game does not show a direct eyewitness or biometric identification.

**Consistency repair:** The brief claimed five people were in the building although the case defines four suspects. It now says four.

## The Last Platform

**Intended solution:** Owen Pike moved the telegram in his dispatch satchel; the parcel-yard contract benefiting his brother supplies the motive.

**Reasoning chain:** The telegram was confirmed in the cabinet at 11:37 and the last dispatch left at 11:40. Blue wax from the sealed telegram is ground into the satchel’s inner strap, a plausible concealment route. The staff ticket under the cabinet is issued only to Owen, and the matching booklet is kept in his compartment. The contract awards the adjacent parcel yard to his brother if the passenger station closes. The continuous signal log places Jules at the telegraph desk, while the fast platform clock prevents its displayed time from being used as a reliable alibi.

| Suspect | Opportunity | Motive | Alibi / contradiction | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Iris Bell | Stationmaster with access to the booking hall | Wants the station to stay open | Two passengers saw her in the booking hall | Motive points against delaying the closure notice; no satchel/ticket trace |
| Owen Pike | Conductor handles the dispatch satchel; staff ticket issued to him | Brother’s parcel yard gains the closure contract | Train inspection sheet has no time or witness signature | Unique combination of route and financial benefit |
| Jules Fenn | Transmitted the telegram and knew its contents | Partner would lose a job if station closes | Continuous signal output log from 11:34 to 11:44 | Strong alibi during the cabinet-to-departure window |
| Vera Dunn | Porter was seen near cabinet | Family boarding house depends on passenger traffic | Luggage tag is not a reliable location record | Nearby, but no evidence links her to the conductor ticket or satchel |

**Alternatives and assumptions:** The ticket issuance record and satchel trace make Owen the uniquely supported culprit in the authored evidence. The case relies on the staff book being accurate and on the dispatch route being under the conductor’s control; both are stated as case facts. The platform clock is eleven minutes fast, but the station bell and signal log use actual time.

## The Blue Hour Score

**Intended solution:** Celia Ward made the replacement with the rehearsal duplicator before Nina sealed the library; the advance draft supplies the motive.

**Reasoning chain:** The original was present at rehearsal’s end. The copy’s carbon dust matches the rehearsal duplicator’s ribbon in Celia’s workroom. Its machine counter advances at 9:36, and the workroom access log records Celia entering at 9:35. Nina seals the library at 9:48, so the swap can occur before the intact seal is applied. The advance draft’s carbon impression names Celia, while the estate note shows the sale was unauthorized. Celia had prepared rehearsal copies and handled the score, providing access to the source material.

| Suspect | Opportunity | Motive | Alibi / contradiction | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Celia Ward | Prepared copies and entered the workroom at 9:35; swap window precedes 9:48 lockup | Draft’s carbon impression names her as recipient of a £12,000 advance | Says she left after rehearsal; workroom log contradicts this | Unique copying-process and motive combination |
| Marco Senn | Had direct access to the score during rehearsal | Wanted the premiere postponed | Radio interview only places him at the station from 10:30 onward | Has a reason but no link to the duplicator/workroom; timing alone does not exclude him |
| Nina Ross | Controls library keys and catalog | Wanted publication delayed | Locks at 9:48; key remains in cabinet and no return is logged | Could access the score before lockup, but no copying or financial clue links her to the replacement |
| Peter Vale | Publisher’s representative; rights negotiation | Company loses exclusive rights if a rival publishes first | Hotel key card records him there from 10:02 onward | Could have been present earlier, but no evidence ties him to Celia’s duplicator or advance |

**Consistency repairs:** The original brief said the score was still in the library at midnight, which contradicted the machine’s 9:36 run and Nina’s 9:48 lockup. It now says the score was present at rehearsal’s end. The copy process previously referred to a typewriter ribbon while also describing a copy-machine counter; it now consistently describes the rehearsal duplicator and its carbon ribbon. The brief now describes the four people as having access to the score or library that evening, not all entering after rehearsal.

**Remaining narrative limit:** The access log and carbon impression are treated as reliable records. A story revision could add explicit provenance (how the access log and recipient impression were authenticated) if the game is intended to support more forensic debate.
